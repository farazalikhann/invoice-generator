Consulting Invoice Template

Consulting invoices usually land on someone else's accounts payable desk, not a personal inbox — which means they need to hold up to scrutiny. A vague "Consulting services: $4,200" line raises questions; a breakdown of hours, rate, and deliverables gets approved and paid without a follow-up email.

The template below starts with a common mix — hourly strategy work, a half-day workshop, and a fixed-fee deliverable — so you can see how to combine time-based and project-based billing on one invoice. Adjust the rates and scope to match your engagement, then export a PDF ready for the client's finance team.

What to include on a consulting invoice

  • Engagement or statement-of-work (SOW) reference
  • Billable hours with the period they cover, or a work summary
  • Hourly rate, or the fixed fee for each deliverable
  • Reimbursable expenses (travel, materials), itemized separately
  • Purchase order (PO) number, if the client's finance team requires one
  • Payment terms (e.g. Net 30) and late payment policy

Consulting invoice FAQ

What payment terms are standard for consulting invoices?

Net 30 is the most common default in B2B consulting, especially with larger clients whose accounts payable process runs on a monthly cycle. Independent consultants working with smaller clients often use Net 15 instead. State the term clearly on the invoice, and consider a late fee (commonly 1-1.5% per month) for overdue balances.

Should I invoice hourly work weekly, biweekly, or monthly?

Monthly is standard for ongoing advisory engagements and keeps the paperwork manageable for both sides. For short, intensive engagements, weekly or biweekly invoicing keeps cash flow steady and avoids one large invoice at the end. Match your cadence to what you agreed in the statement of work.

How do I invoice for a fixed-fee project with milestones?

Break the total fee into line items tied to each milestone or deliverable (for example, 30% on kickoff, 40% on draft delivery, 30% on final sign-off) rather than one lump sum. That way each invoice is self-explanatory and matches what's been completed at the time it's sent.
