What to include on a freelance writer invoice

  • Publication or client name and the specific piece(s) delivered
  • Word count or hours worked, and the pricing basis (per word, per hour, or flat)
  • Delivery or publication date for each piece
  • Revision rounds included versus billed separately
  • Usage or licensing terms (one-time use, exclusive rights, syndication)
  • Late payment terms, if you charge interest on overdue invoices

Freelance writer invoice FAQ

Should I invoice per article or in a monthly batch?

Either works — match it to the relationship. For a single one-off piece, invoice as soon as it's delivered or published. For an ongoing client sending you regular assignments, a monthly batch invoice with one line per piece is usually easier for both sides to track.

Should I charge by the word, the hour, or a flat project fee?

It depends on the work: per-word rates suit straightforward articles, hourly suits research-heavy or open-ended projects, and flat fees suit well-scoped deliverables like a landing page or a content strategy document. Whichever you pick, state the basis on the invoice line item so the math is transparent.

What's a kill fee and should I include it on an invoice?

A kill fee is a partial payment (commonly 25-50% of the agreed rate) for work you completed on an assignment a client ultimately doesn't publish or use. If your contract includes one, list it as its own line item — for example "Kill fee, unpublished article" — rather than folding it into another charge.
